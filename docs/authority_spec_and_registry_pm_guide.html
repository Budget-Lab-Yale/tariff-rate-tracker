Policy instructions and model plumbing are two different things.

AuthoritySpec tells the model what a tariff does. The authority registry tells the software where that tariff belongs. They work together, but neither replaces the other.

✓ Short answer: the registry is not AuthoritySpec.

How one legal fact becomes a published number

The registry does not calculate a tariff. It connects the policy description to the common calculation and reporting machinery.

1

Source facts

Law, proclamation, HTS material, and approved policy parameters.

2

Adapter

Translates differently shaped source material into one common format.

3

AuthoritySpec

The policy instruction: scope, rates, dates, exemptions, interactions.

4

Registry lookup

Connects the spec to rate, net, schema, and reporting plumbing.

5

Rate panel

One product × country table with a column for each authority.

6

Public outputs

Snapshots, daily rates, country views, product views, and authority views.

Key control point: the AuthoritySpec gives the calculator its policy instructions. The registry ensures that the calculated number is carried consistently through the system.

What the two data structures look like

AuthoritySpec is nested because policy is nested. The registry is flat because it is a directory of software connections.

  • Write rate: rate_s338

What changes at the effective date

18 August 2026

Not active yet

The §338 spec has no active Canada rate. Covered rows carry rate_s338 = 0.

19 AUG
policy boundary
19 August 2026

Active

Covered Canadian rows carry rate_s338 = 0.50. Because the rule is additive, net_s338 is also 0.50 unless an upstream scope rule excludes the row.

Important nuance: some §338 exclusions are legal scope rules applied before stacking—for example, certain §232 overlaps. The registry does not attempt to restate those legal rules; they remain in reviewable policy code and AuthoritySpec-related logic.

Division of responsibility: AuthoritySpec can carry the policy's stacking class. The registry connects that class to the correct rate and net columns and supplies the established default when needed. The stacker performs the arithmetic once.

How the design fails safely

Centralization helps only if bad entries are rejected. The registry is validated when it loads.

⛔
Duplicate rate or net columns

Rejected instead of letting two authorities overwrite or impersonate each other.

⛔
Unknown stacking class

Rejected instead of silently choosing an unintended combination rule.

⛔
Broken panel order

Rejected so published columns do not drift unpredictably.

⛔
Missing schema placement

Rejected for baseline authorities so a live tariff cannot vanish from saved output.

Migration evidence: 37/37 local test files passed; the production verifier passed 11/11; baseline and new_301 matched the sealed reference on all 120 artifacts. The restructuring changed no published numbers.

Questions a PM might ask

Is the authority registry an AuthoritySpec?

No. AuthoritySpec is a policy object produced for an authority and date. The registry is a static software directory that connects those objects to common columns and machinery.

Is either one an external configuration file?

No. Both structures are implemented in version-controlled R code. AuthoritySpec may contain facts drawn from HTS material and approved policy parameters, but the registry itself is not a new configuration system.

Why not put everything into one giant object?

Because policy and plumbing change for different reasons. Rates and dates change when policy changes; column wiring changes when the software adds a new authority. Separating them makes each easier to review.

Does registering a tariff make it legally correct?

No. Registration prevents software-wiring omissions. Legal scope, dates, rates, exceptions, and interactions still require policy review and tests.

What should happen when the next tariff arrives?

Create or adapt its AuthoritySpec, add one registry row for shared plumbing, implement any genuinely new policy interaction, add legal examples, and run the full production parity/correctness gates.

The management takeaway

AuthoritySpec makes policy explicit. The registry makes software wiring consistent. Together they reduce the chance that a correctly interpreted tariff is implemented in one calculation but forgotten in a schema, scenario, or report.
